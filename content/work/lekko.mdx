  Lekko is a dynamic configuration and feature-flag platform (later acquired by Rippling). My role: diagnose and redesign the developer onboarding flow that was producing a 34% day-1 drop-off rate. Outcome: drop-off reduced to 11%, time-to-first-value cut in half.

## The activation problem

Lekko's product is genuinely good. The thesis is compelling: ship a feature flag SDK, give developers the ability to change product behavior without deploying code. Engineers who get it, love it.

But they weren't getting it.

The onboarding drop-off was brutal. One in three developers who signed up never made it to first value — which in Lekko's case meant creating and evaluating their first feature flag. They were bouncing in the first 24 hours.

## Finding the real blockers

I ran a heuristic audit of the existing onboarding flow, then layered in session recordings from 40 users and exit surveys from those who churned in day one.

Three failure points dominated:

**The SDK installation step.** Lekko supported six SDKs across multiple languages. The onboarding flow presented them all simultaneously. Developers — who by nature scan for the fastest path — were stalling on the decision. Choice paralysis on step one.

**The "first flag" prompt.** After installing the SDK, users were asked to create a flag with no context about *what* they were building or *why* it would be useful in their specific stack. Blank canvas, no scaffolding, no example.

**The API key step.** Buried in step three, presented in a wall of copy, no visual separation. A non-trivial percentage of users were getting to this step and abandoning because they couldn't find their key without navigating away.

## The redesign

I rebuilt the onboarding around a single principle: **get the developer to a working flag as fast as possible, in their stack, with their own code.**

**SDK selection:** Added a stack detector that pre-selected the SDK based on the developer's repo language (detectable via OAuth). Removed the six-option grid. Surfaced one option with a "Change" link.

**First flag scaffolding:** Introduced a flag template library — three pre-built flag patterns (kill switch, A/B test, gradual rollout) with code snippets in the user's language. Let them start from something real.

**API key step:** Redesigned as a dedicated, visually isolated step. Added a copy-to-clipboard button and a persistent sidebar showing where to find the key without leaving the flow.

## The acquisition footnote

Lekko was acquired by Rippling. The onboarding work was part of the product surface that got evaluated during due diligence. I'm not going to overclaim causality. But I'll take the data point.

## What I took from it

Developer onboarding is a different problem than consumer onboarding. Developers are fast and opinionated and they will bounce the moment they feel like the product is wasting their time. The standard UX playbook — progressive disclosure, friendly copy, visual hierarchy — still applies. But the bar for "wasted time" is much lower. Every unnecessary choice, every ambiguous step, every moment where they have to leave the flow to find something — those are exits waiting to happen.

Speed to value is the only metric that matters on day one.
