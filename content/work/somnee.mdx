<Callout>
  Somnee is a sleep headband that uses transcranial electrical stimulation to improve sleep onset and quality. My role: redesign the companion app experience to close the feedback loop between the device's clinical efficacy and the user's understanding of it. Outcome: 40% improvement in 30-day retention and a measurable increase in nightly usage consistency.
</Callout>

## The problem with invisible products

Sleep tech has a fundamental UX problem: the product does its most important work while the user is unconscious.

You can't see the stimulation. You can't feel it working. You wake up and you either feel rested or you don't — and if you don't, you have no idea whether the device helped, hurt, or was irrelevant to your night.

This creates a trust vacuum. And in that vacuum, users stop using the device.

<PullQuote>
  The product was working. Users just didn't know it.
</PullQuote>

## What I found in the data

We had biometric data. Detailed, timestamped, scientifically meaningful biometric data. And we were showing users almost none of it.

The app had a sleep score — a single number that collapsed hours of signal into one dimensionless metric. Users didn't trust it because they didn't know what was behind it. And because they didn't trust it, they didn't engage with it. And because they didn't engage with it, they didn't build a habit around the device.

I ran a survey of 120 users, followed by eight in-depth interviews. The pattern was consistent: users wanted to understand *why* they slept the way they did, not just *how* they scored.

## The redesign

I rebuilt the post-sleep experience around three principles:

1. **Attribution before score.** Before the user sees a number, they see what happened. Two to three factors that meaningfully affected their sleep, in plain language.

2. **Progress over perfection.** Instead of comparing each night to an abstract ideal, show the user their own trend. Week over week. Month over month. Progress is more motivating than gap.

3. **Device credit.** Explicitly surface the delta between nights using Somnee and nights without. Make the device's contribution visible.

## What changed

The redesign didn't add new features. It changed what the existing features communicated.

That's a distinction worth making. A lot of consumer health product work defaults to feature addition — more charts, more data, more settings. The users I talked to didn't want more. They wanted clarity. They wanted to feel like the product understood their night and had something to say about it.

When we gave them that, they kept coming back.
